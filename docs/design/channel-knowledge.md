# Channel knowledge (Ask)

Status: **Built** ([ADR 0022](../adr/0022-ask-channel-knowledge.md)). User guide: [Ask](../guides/ask.md).

Ask indexes what YouTube channels say in their videos (captions only), lets anyone search it, and answers questions about it in the chat panel with a numbered reference to the moment for every point. Code: `backend/app/services/knowledge/`, `backend/app/api/ask.py`, the **Channels Ask can answer from** section of Settings → Library (`frontend/src/pages/settings/AskChannelsSection.tsx`, `frontend/src/pages/ask/`), the **Said in videos** section of home search results (`frontend/src/pages/library/SaidInVideos.tsx`), the Ask panel (`components/HelpChatPanel.tsx`).

## Data (revision `0022_ask_knowledge`)

| Table | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `kb_channels` | One per Ask channel: canonical URL (unique), name, `include_streams` (default off), `max_videos` (null = all), `status` (`queued` / `listing` / `indexing` / `ready` / `paused` / `error`), the embedding model and dimension of its vectors, `added_by` (`SET NULL`) |
| `kb_videos` | One per upload: YouTube id, title, description, date, `is_stream`, `position` (0 = newest), `fetch_status` (`pending` / `fetched` / `no_captions` / `members_only` / `failed`), attempts and next attempt, `caption_auto`, `cues` (zlib JSON of `[start_sec, text]` lines of about 5 seconds), `embedded_model` |
| `kb_chunks` | Passages: `start_sec`, `end_sec`, `text`; `chunk_index` -1 is the title and description. `kb_channel_id` is denormalised for filtering |
| `kb_chunks_fts` | FTS5, external content on `kb_chunks.text`; triggers `trg_kb_chunks_fts_insert` / `_update` (`AFTER UPDATE OF text`) / `_delete` use the external-content `'delete'` form |
| `kb_chunk_vectors` | `chunk_id` → float16 vector |
| `ask_threads`, `ask_messages` | Each user's saved Ask conversations (answers from channels, a video and Horde), with each answer's references, video or doc sources as JSON; `scope` keeps the last source (`channels`, `video` or `help`) |

`ai_jobs.kb_channel_id` serves the `embed_kb_channel` job. Nothing references the channel catalog: it trims beyond its cap, merges duplicates by deleting rows, and can be switched off. Progress counts are `COUNT` queries, never stored. Factory reset (erase media) deletes these tables.

## Indexing

The `ask` worker (`knowledge/worker.py`, in the `WorkerRegistry`, off under `HORDE_E2E`) does one unit of work per pass:

1. **List** a channel that is new, failed listing an hour ago, or was listed a day ago: `fetch_channel_feed` for the Videos tab (and Live when `include_streams`), 100 per page, inside `call_gated` at background priority (`knowledge/listing.py`). Shorts are never listed; upcoming and live broadcasts are skipped. Rows that disappear from a listing are kept.
2. **Fetch** one video's captions, oldest-added channel first, newest upload first (`knowledge/captions.py`): from the library copy's caption file when there is one, otherwise a subtitle-only yt-dlp call in the extract gate (`downloads/subtitles.download_with_subtitles`, which keeps a video's own captions over a dubbed audio track's captions translated back, see below). The call also returns the description and the upload date; the listing gives no exact date, so `kb_videos.published_at` is set here. A YouTube fetch runs only when no library caption retry is due and `TimedtextPacer.try_reserve_background(15)` succeeds; a rate limit (`RETRY`) backs the video off and blocks every caption fetch (`services/timedtext.py`). Other failures retry three times, an hour apart and growing.
3. **Chunk** (`knowledge/chunking.py`): `chapters.caption_lines` merges YouTube's rolling captions into 5-second lines that hold each word once; passages gather lines up to 75 seconds or 1,400 characters and start the next 15 seconds before the end.
4. **Embed** (`knowledge/embed.py`): after fetches and on idle passes, an `embed_kb_channel` job is queued for any channel with passages not embedded with the current model. A job embeds 25 videos (text prefixed with channel, title and year), commits before each provider call, and queues the next batch. `ai/worker._next_job` picks library jobs first. Only videos embedded with the channel's model are searched, so changing the model re-embeds without mixing vector spaces.

Removing a channel deletes its passages in batches of 500 (the FTS triggers fire per row) before the channel; turning streams off deletes stream videos 50 at a time. The worker re-reads rows after every network call, so a channel removed or paused meanwhile is skipped. A listing cut off by a restart starts over (`worker.recover`).

## Search

`knowledge/search.py` builds every MATCH expression itself: each term is a quoted phrase (a lone word of four letters or more also matches its endings), stop words are dropped, terms are ORed and ranked by `bm25`, always with a `LIMIT`. Hostile input never reaches FTS5 syntax. `GET /api/ask/search` serves the **Said in videos** section of the home page's search results (every Ask channel; shown once one has passages); it links each hit to the caption line within the passage that shares the most words with the query (`knowledge/moments.py`), two seconds early.

## Chat

`POST /api/ask/chat` (`knowledge/ask_chat.py`) streams one turn:

1. **Source** (`knowledge/routing.py`): a question about Ask's indexing (`routing.about_ask_channels`: "is X indexed?") → Horde, even when it names a channel; else `answer_from` ("Ask Horde instead" or "Ask \<video\> instead" under an answer) → that source for this turn, whatever the question names; else "all my channels" → every channel with passages; channels the question names (name or handle, with or without "the", spaces or an `'s`; initials of three or more words only in a "what does X say" question, `routing.asks_what_said`) → those; else the `channel_ids` the client sends (opened with "Ask <channel>", or the previous answer's channels) → those; else, with a video (`video`, picked, or `page_video`, the watch page's; `resolve_video` drops it when video chat is off, and a downloaded stream becomes its library copy): Horde when `routing.names_horde` matches or `help_data.topics` does and `routing.about_video` does not; the `page_channel_id` when `routing.about_channel` matches ("this channel", "his other videos") with the watched video's passages ranked first; Horde when `routing.about_horde` matches and `about_video` does not (for a picked video, only with `routing.asks_how_to`); else the video. A picked video stays in the thread's `scope` whatever answers, and a new thread about a video is titled with it; without one, the `page_channel_id` (a channel page) unless the question is about Horde (`routing.about_horde`, with `routing.asks_how_to` or without `routing.about_video`: "how do I download his latest video?" is Horde's) → that channel; else Horde. A question that names no channel and reaches Horde gets a note listing the indexed channels (`help_note._topic_note`), and with one or two indexed channels the panel offers **Ask \<channel\> instead** under the answer. The route picks this before streaming (`ask_chat.pick_route`), and saves a stream's row first (`api/stream_ai.prepared_state`; if that fails for the page's video, the turn is routed again without it). Events `thread` and `scope` (with `channels` and `video`) say which, and the source beside the panel's title follows `scope` unless a video is picked.
2. **A video** runs `ai.chat.run_chat_turn` with the Assistant model and a target (`_VideoChat`) whose context is the per-video chat's (`ai/embeddings.build_chat_context` for a library video: details, matching caption passages, saved comments; `stream_ai.chat_context` for a stream) and which saves both turns in the thread, the answer with `sources` `{"kind": "video", "video": …}`.
3. **Horde** runs `help_chat.stream_help_events` with the saved history and a store that writes both turns to the thread. A question about indexing (`routing.about_ask_channels`) or about what a channel says (`routing.asks_what_said`) gets `knowledge/help_note.for_question`: every Ask channel with its status and progress (anyone may list them), and for each library channel it names that is not indexed, a note saying so, with that channel's library videos when the user allows their data.
4. **Channels**: check the AI is allowed and the Assistant model present before any cost; keep the channels with the most matching passages when there are more than the model compares (two for small, four otherwise). Then (`knowledge/retrieval.py`):
    - **Expand** the question with one JSON call (skipped for small models): `terms` (spellings captions use, generations, part names), `subjects` (the products, people or works it is about, as a title spells them), `search` (the question as one sentence) and `recent` (the answer depends on what is newest). The call sees the last four messages, so a follow-up ("what about its battery?") is expanded with what it refers to. Without an expansion, a short or back-pointing follow-up is searched together with the user's previous question (`search_question`).
    - **Watching**: asked from a watch page whose video the channel has, the expansion and the answer are told its title, so "this device" means what it is about (`retrieval.watched_title`).
    - **Search** each channel: keyword and vector rankings, plus, counted twice, a keyword ranking of only the videos whose title names a subject (the battery passage of the Odin 3 review need not say "Odin 3"), fused by reciprocal rank (k = 60). When the question is about what is current (`recent`, or words such as "right now", "latest"), each score is scaled by how new its video is among the channel's uploads, up to 1.6 times for the newest.
    - **Pick** the best videos' best non-overlapping passages (three per video; ten, ranked first, for a video whose whole title is in the question: `retrieval.named_videos`) within a fair share of the budget: 6,000 / 12,000 / 20,000 characters by model tier for a model on this network, 12,000 / 36,000 / 60,000 for a hosted one (`ask_chat._budget`). A question about the channel's newest videos ("what are his latest videos about?") first takes the title and description of its ten newest.
    - **Format** the excerpts numbered in date order, without times, under one heading per video: channel, title, upload date and the first paragraph of the description, which spells the names captions garble.
   Nothing found → a fixed answer, no model call.
5. The model (`ask_chat.system_prompt`) answers with thinking off (`think=False`, OpenRouter's `reasoning: {enabled: false}`; the query expansion too): a reasoning model thinking first was three times slower, and its thinking used the whole token budget, so the expansion JSON came back cut off and some answers empty. It is told today's date, that a later video supersedes an earlier one (a year-end roundup a mid-year one), to answer first and combine the excerpts, to go by the newest videos for what is current and say when an older one differs, to treat an excerpt as being about what its video's title names, to spell names as titles and descriptions do, to say in one sentence when the videos do not cover the question, and to cite `[n]` only and never write times. A follow-up is spelled out for it, and earlier answers reach it without their markers (they were numbered against other excerpts). Its answer is resolved before it is saved (`knowledge/references.py`): numbers it was not given are dropped, the rest renumbered in order of first use, each resolved to the caption line its sentence matches (an excerpt cited for two different moments becomes two references). A `references` event precedes `done`; the expansion call's cost is added to the turn's.

The panel hides the model's raw numbers while streaming and renders the final markers as superscript links (`AiMarkdown`'s `cite` hook) with a reference list (channel, title, year, timestamp, the caption line) folded behind an "N references" disclosure. Links open `/watch/<id>?t=` for a downloaded copy, else `/watch?url=…&t=`; the watch page reads `t`, keeps it through the stream-to-library redirect and seeks in place when that video is open.

## Permissions

`manage_ask` (off by default) adds, changes, pauses, resumes and removes channels; everyone signed in lists channels and searches; `use_ai` chats and reads, lists and deletes their own threads. Members see a generic error instead of YouTube's text.

## Sizes and limits

A 500-video channel of 25-minute videos is about 12,000 passages and about 45 MB (13 MB text, 10 MB FTS, 18 MB float16 vectors at 768 dimensions, a few MB of lines), copied by 7 nightly and 5 pre-migration backups. The vector cache keeps 8 channels. English captions only (the video's own: YouTube also offers every dubbed audio track's captions translated to English, which yt-dlp would pick and YouTube rate limits at once); videos without captions are listed but only their title and description are searchable.
